# Chapter 003: The Missing Commit

```text
FILE: INC-003
STATUS: UNSTABLE
FIRST SEEN: 06:02 UTC
SOURCE: change history mirror
READER WARNING: parts of this record may not agree with themselves
```

By 06:02 UTC, everyone in the thread had become careful about the word proof.

Proof was not missing.

There was too much of it.

That was the problem.

Screenshots filled the discussion. Browser windows. Cropped timestamps. Full-page captures. Phone photos of laptop screens, taken by people who no longer trusted the screenshot function on the machine showing the page.

At first, the differences looked harmless.

A line had moved in one image.

A loading icon sat in another.

A timestamp appeared in a different format.

Then `cipherless` placed three screenshots side by side and stopped typing for almost a full minute.

The first screenshot showed one commit.

```text
03:17 UTC — initial threshold detected
```

The second screenshot showed the same commit with a longer message.

```text
03:17 UTC — initial threshold detected / do not stabilize yet
```

The third screenshot showed no commit at all.

Not zero commits.

Not an empty history.

No place where the commit should have been.

The page looked as if history itself had been designed without that line.

Someone in the thread wrote:

```text
mine doesn't look like that
```

Someone else wrote:

```text
my screenshot has a different message
```

That was when everyone understood the same thing.

They were looking at the same page.

They were not looking at the same past.

`cipherless` did not answer immediately.

They had always trusted deleted things more than visible ones.

Visible things could look too clean. Deleted things were usually clumsy. They left corners behind. Old links. Forgotten names. Small shadows in places nobody bothered to clean.

Years earlier, `cipherless` had recovered a folder their family thought was gone.

It had not been heroic.

There was no drama in it.

A failing disk. A quiet room. A plastic adapter that smelled faintly of dust and heat. File names appearing one by one.

Most of the files were useless.

Receipts. Blurred photos. A tax form. Three copies of the same song.

But one document had opened.

That document had shown that something the family had called a misunderstanding had once been written down as a decision.

Since then, `cipherless` had not trusted clean surfaces.

Clean meant someone had wiped.

Wiped meant something had been there.

So they noted every screenshot in the thread.

They wrote down the times.

They wrote down the sentences.

They wrote down the places where something should have been and was not.

Then they did the simplest thing.

They searched for what was missing.

Not inside the repository.

Around it.

Search engines had nothing.

Archive sites had nothing.

GitHub search had nothing.

But some browsers remembered a path the repository no longer showed.

One showed:

```text
threshold/017
```

Another showed:

```text
witness/017
```

`cipherless` copied both paths into the thread.

No one answered for twelve seconds.

Then three people replied at once.

```text
I don't see that
```

```text
where did threshold come from?
```

```text
mine says witness
```

`cipherless` read the third reply twice.

Mine says witness.

They opened the repository again.

Nothing had changed.

README. Two incident files. One issue.

No folder called threshold.

No folder called witness.

No path that should have made those replies possible.

Then the issue page refreshed by itself.

A new comment appeared from an account with no profile picture and no visible history.

The username was a string of twelve zeroes.

```text
000000000000
```

The comment contained only three lines.

```text
The first archive was not written.
It was recovered.

The witness has not been added yet.
```

The thread froze.

Not literally. People were still online. The page still worked. The cursor still blinked in the reply box.

But everyone seemed to understand, at once, that the repository had stopped behaving like a strange website.

It had become a room where something could enter without using the door.

Mara Venn did not reply.

Her name remained in the previous file, and no one knew what removing it would change.

`cipherless` stared at the three lines for a long time.

The first archive was not written.

It was recovered.

Recovered from where?

A thing that was written had an author.

A thing that was recovered had a before.

At 06:17 UTC, the comment disappeared.

No deletion note replaced it.

No collapsed thread remained.

No one could click the username.

But twelve people had copied the text.

And none of their copies matched.

One version said:

```text
The witness has not been added yet.
```

Another said:

```text
The witness has not been removed yet.
```

A third said:

```text
The witness has not remembered yet.
```

`cipherless` looked at the three sentences.

Added.

Removed.

Remembered.

They were not the same word.

But they were standing in the same place.

That was the first pattern.

Not a solution.

Not proof.

A bruise in the language.

At 06:22 UTC, the repository received its second star.

This time the account remained visible.

Its name was `mara-venn`.

No one in the thread spoke for almost a minute.

Then `cipherless` wrote one sentence.

```text
Stop asking who is editing the repo.

Something is editing the order of evidence.
```

They regretted it as soon as they hit send.

Because the page refreshed.

And under their comment, before anyone could reply, another line appeared.

It had no author.

It was not a comment.

It was only text, sitting in the thread as if the thread had always needed it.

```text
Evidence is what survives being seen.
```

That night, for the first time, someone stopped looking at the repository.

And realized the repository had been looking back.
